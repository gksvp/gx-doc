# Granite Slab Measurement Guide

How GKSVP measures each slab, how to read the sizes on a listing, and how to work out how much granite your project needs.

---

## 1. Words used in this guide

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Length** | The longer side of the slab |
| **Height** (or width) | The shorter side of the slab |
| **Thickness** | Depth of the slab: front face to back |
| **Gross size** | The full outer size, edge to edge |
| **Net size** (usable size) | The largest clean rectangle you can cut, after leaving out broken corners, chipped edges and rough ends |
| **Area** | Length × height, in square feet (sq ft) or square metres (m²) |
| **Lot / bundle** | Slabs cut one after another from the same block. They match most closely in colour and pattern. |

---

## 2. How GKSVP measures a slab

Every slab sold on GKSVP is measured and photographed on its own, and has its own slab number on its label.

1. **Length and height** are measured on the polished face with a steel tape, at the **shortest** point along each side. Slab edges are seldom perfectly straight, so the shortest reading is the safe one.
2. **Net size** is recorded as the largest clean rectangle: broken corners and chipped or uneven edges are left out.
3. **Thickness** is measured with a vernier caliper at several points around the edge, and the average and the thinnest reading are noted.
4. **Area** is worked out from the net size, so the area you pay for is the area you can use.

The listing shows the net length × height, the thickness, the area and the slab photos. If a slab has a natural feature or repair (see the [Quality Inspection Guide](quality-inspection-guide.md)), the listing says so.

## 3. Working out the area

### From inches

```
Area (sq ft) = Length (in) × Height (in) ÷ 144
```

Example: a slab 108 in × 60 in
108 × 60 = 6,480 sq in → 6,480 ÷ 144 = **45 sq ft**

### From feet and inches

Turn everything into inches first. Example: 9 ft 2 in × 5 ft 1 in
9 ft 2 in = 110 in, 5 ft 1 in = 61 in → 110 × 61 ÷ 144 = **46.6 sq ft**

### From centimetres

```
Area (m²)    = Length (cm) × Height (cm) ÷ 10,000
Area (sq ft) = Area (m²) × 10.764
```

Example: 275 cm × 155 cm
275 × 155 = 42,625 cm² → 4.26 m² → 4.26 × 10.764 = **45.9 sq ft**

### Handy conversions

| From | To | Multiply by |
|---|---|---|
| inches | centimetres | 2.54 |
| feet | metres | 0.3048 |
| square metres | square feet | 10.764 |
| square feet | square metres | 0.0929 |

## 4. Thickness

Common granite thicknesses:

| Thickness | Typical use |
|---|---|
| 15–16 mm | Wall cladding, light-use tops |
| 18–20 mm | Kitchen and bathroom tops, flooring, stair treads and risers |
| 30 mm | Heavy-duty kitchen tops, long overhangs, outdoor paving, steps |

Slabs are sawn from natural stone, so thickness varies slightly across one slab. Check the thinnest reading on the listing when your fitting needs an exact depth (for example under a sink clip or a door frame).

### Weight

Granite weighs roughly 2,600–2,700 kg per cubic metre. As a guide:

| Thickness | Approx. weight per m² | Approx. weight per sq ft |
|---|---|---|
| 18 mm | 48 kg | 4.4 kg |
| 20 mm | 53 kg | 4.9 kg |
| 30 mm | 80 kg | 7.4 kg |

A 45 sq ft slab at 18 mm weighs about 200 kg. Plan for enough people or lifting equipment, and see the safety notes in the [Installation Guide](slab-installation-guide.md).

## 5. Measuring your project

### Kitchen top

1. Measure each run of counter **along the wall**, from corner to end, in inches or centimetres.
2. Measure the **depth** from the wall to where the front edge should be. Add the front overhang you want beyond the cabinet (commonly about 1 inch / 25 mm).
3. Note every **cut-out**: sink, hob, tap holes. Write the model numbers; the fitter needs the maker's cut-out template.
4. Add **backsplash** strips if you want them: length × height of each.
5. Draw a simple sketch with every measurement, and mark which walls are not square.

Example: L-shaped kitchen

| Piece | Size | Area |
|---|---|---|
| Run A | 120 in × 26 in | 21.7 sq ft |
| Run B | 84 in × 26 in | 15.2 sq ft |
| Backsplash | 204 in × 4 in | 5.7 sq ft |
| **Total** | | **42.5 sq ft** |

Cut-outs are **not** subtracted: the stone is still bought and then cut.

### Floor

1. Measure the room's length and width at the floor, wall to wall.
2. Multiply for the area. Split L-shaped rooms into rectangles and add them.
3. Add door thresholds, steps and skirting separately.

### Stairs

Measure each **tread** (the step you stand on: width × depth, including the nosing) and each **riser** (the upright face: width × height). Count how many of each.

## 6. How much extra to order

Some stone is always lost to saw cuts, pattern matching, corners and joins. How much depends on the layout and on whether the pattern must line up across joints. As a starting point many fitters allow:

| Job | Extra on top of the measured area |
|---|---|
| Simple straight kitchen top, plain stone | about 10% |
| L- or U-shaped top, or stone with strong veins to match | about 15–20% |
| Floors with many cuts, borders or diagonal laying | about 10–15% |

Ask your fitter to lay the pieces out on the actual slab sizes before you buy; that gives the real number. GKSVP's support team can also check a layout against specific slabs.

**Tip:** buy every slab for one area **from the same lot**. Slabs from different blocks of the same granite can differ noticeably in shade and pattern.
