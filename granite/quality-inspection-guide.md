# Granite Quality Inspection Guide

How to check a granite slab before you accept it: what is a natural feature, what is a defect, and the simple tests anyone can do on site with a few tools.

---

## 1. What you need

- Good light: daylight, or a torch you can hold at a low angle to the surface
- Steel tape and a vernier caliper
- A straightedge at least 1 m (3 ft) long, ideally 2 m
- Clean water, a dropper or spoon, and a dry cloth
- Cotton swabs and acetone (nail-polish remover)
- A coin or a small metal key
- Phone camera, to photograph anything you want to raise

Inspect slabs **standing on an A-frame or rack**, not lying flat in a pile, and clean the face first: dust hides scratches and dull patches.

---

## 2. Natural features vs defects

Granite is a natural stone. Some variation is part of its character and is not a fault.

| Usually acceptable (natural) | Usually a defect |
|---|---|
| Colour and grain varying slightly across the slab | A crack you can catch with a fingernail, or that shows on the back too |
| Mineral clusters, darker or lighter "flowers" | Open pits or holes in the polished face that collect dirt |
| Fine, closed fissures that don't open under a fingernail and don't show on the back | Chipped edges or broken corners inside the usable area |
| Small pits that were filled with colour-matched resin during polishing | Dull, hazy patches where the polish didn't take |
| Differences between slabs from **different** lots | Clear shade difference between slabs sold as one lot |
| A mesh or resin backing on the back of the slab, put on at the factory to strengthen it | Rust stains, or a smell of chemicals / oily feel from surface treatment |

When in doubt, compare with the listing photos. On GKSVP every slab is photographed individually, so what you receive should match what you chose.

## 3. Step-by-step inspection

### Step 1: Match the slab to your order

- Read the **slab number** on the label (or scan its QR code) and check it matches your order.
- Compare the slab with its listing photos: colour, veins, any marked feature.
- For several slabs, check they are from the **same lot** and are numbered in sequence if you need the pattern to flow.

### Step 2: Measure

- Measure length and height at the shortest points (see the [Measurement Guide](slab-measurement-guide.md)).
- Measure thickness with the caliper at 4–6 points around the edge. Note the thinnest and thickest readings.
- Compare with the listing.

### Step 3: Look across the surface

- Hold a torch low, almost flat to the surface, and look along the slab from each end. Low light shows scratches, pits, dull patches and uneven polish that you won't see from the front.
- Look at the slab from a few metres away for colour patches or bands that would stand out once installed.

### Step 4: Check flatness

- Lay the straightedge on the face lengthwise, across, and corner to corner.
- Look for gaps under it, or slide a sheet of paper under to find them.
- A warped or bowed slab is hard to install flat, especially on floors and long tops. If you see a clear gap, photograph it with a ruler next to it and ask before accepting.

### Step 5: Check for cracks

- Run a fingernail across any line you see. A closed natural fissure feels smooth; a crack catches the nail.
- Look at the **back** of the slab at the same spot. A line that goes right through is a crack.
- **Tap test:** tap gently with a coin or key across the slab. Solid stone gives a clear, ringing sound; a dull or "dead" sound over one area can mean a hidden crack or a loose backing. Check that spot closely.

### Step 6: Water test (absorption)

- Put a few drops of water on the polished face in 3–4 places, including lighter areas.
- Wait about 10–15 minutes, then wipe.
- **Water still beading, no dark spot:** low absorption, typical of good granite.
- **Dark spot that disappears as it dries:** the stone absorbs some water. Fine for most uses, but plan to seal kitchen tops (see the [Installation Guide](slab-installation-guide.md)).
- **Dark spot within a minute or two:** high absorption. Ask the seller before using it in a kitchen or bathroom.

### Step 7: Dye test (mainly black and very dark granites)

Some low-grade dark stone is dyed or treated to look deeper black.

- Rub a cotton swab with acetone firmly on a **hidden spot or an offcut** (the edge or the back) for about 30 seconds.
- **Swab stays clean:** no surface dye.
- **Swab turns black or brown:** colour came off; the stone is likely dyed or oil-treated. Refuse it, or ask the seller.

Don't test on the polished face of a slab you plan to use. Acetone won't harm granite, but a coloured treatment will show where you rubbed.

### Step 8: Edges and corners

- Check every edge for chips and every corner for breaks.
- Small chips outside the usable (net) size don't matter; they are cut away. Chips **inside** the net size do.

### Step 9: Record and report

- Photograph anything you're not happy with, with a tape or ruler in the picture and the slab label visible.
- On GKSVP, raise it from your order page (**Raise concern**) **before** the slab is cut or installed. Once granite is cut or fixed, it usually can't be returned.

## 5. Laboratory tests for large projects

For large or specified projects (building cladding, public spaces, export orders), ask for lab test reports on samples from the same block. Common references:

- **ASTM C615**: *Standard Specification for Granite Dimension Stone* (ASTM International). Its physical requirements table (C615-03 edition) sets, for granite:

  | Property | Requirement | Test method |
  |---|---|---|
  | Absorption by weight | max 0.40% | ASTM C97 |
  | Density | min 2,560 kg/m³ (160 lb/ft³) | ASTM C97 |
  | Compressive strength | min 131 MPa (19,000 psi) | ASTM C170 |
  | Modulus of rupture | min 10.34 MPa (1,500 psi) | ASTM C99 |
  | Flexural strength | min 8.27 MPa (1,200 psi) | ASTM C880 |
  | Abrasion resistance (floors with foot traffic) | min 25 (Ha) | ASTM C241 / C1353 |

  Check the current edition of ASTM C615 for the values that apply to your contract.

- **IS 14223 (Part 1)**: Bureau of Indian Standards specification for polished building stones, granite. Use the current BIS text for its requirements and the IS test methods it refers to.

A test report applies to the samples tested. Ask for reports that name the block or lot your slabs come from.

## 6. After installation

Some things only show up once the stone is fixed and lit by its final lighting. If you see a crack, a colour patch or a lifting tile after installation, photograph it and contact the installer first: most post-installation problems come from the base, supports or fixing rather than the slab (see the [Installation Guide](slab-installation-guide.md)).
