# Granite Slab Installation Guide

How to handle, cut and fit granite for kitchen and bathroom tops, floors and stairs, and how to look after it afterwards. Written for homeowners and site supervisors, so you know what a good installation looks like and what to ask your fitter.

> Granite slabs are heavy and break if handled wrongly. Installation, cutting and polishing should be done by an experienced stone fitter with the right tools.

---

## 1. Safety first

- **Weight:** an 18 mm slab weighs about 48 kg per m², roughly 4.4 kg per sq ft; a full slab often weighs 150–300 kg. Use enough people, slab clamps, trolleys or a crane.
- **Carry slabs on edge, never flat.** A slab carried flat bends under its own weight and cracks. Store and move slabs upright on A-frames, leaning slightly, with wooden or rubber packing under the bottom edge.
- **Cutting dust:** cutting and grinding granite releases fine crystalline silica dust, which damages the lungs. Cut **wet** (water-fed saw and grinder) and wear a suitable dust mask or respirator, eye and ear protection. Don't dry-cut indoors.
- Wear gloves and safety shoes, and keep children and pets away from the work area.

---

## 2. Before installation

### Delivery and storage

- Check every slab on delivery against the [Quality Inspection Guide](quality-inspection-guide.md), **before** anything is cut. A slab that is cut or fixed usually can't be returned.
- Store slabs upright on A-frames, on a flat, firm base, out of the way of traffic. Keep them covered from cement splash and paint.

### Plan the layout

- Lay out where each piece comes from on the actual slabs: veins should run the same way, and joints should fall in sensible places (not over a sink or hob, not in the middle of a short run).
- For floors, lay slabs or tiles out dry first and number them, so colour and pattern blend across the room.
- Make **templates** (hardboard or thin plywood) of the actual cabinets and walls. Walls are rarely straight; templates avoid gaps.

---

## 3. Kitchen and bathroom tops

### The base

- Cabinets must be **level and firmly fixed** to the wall and to each other. Check with a spirit level along and across, and pack under the cabinet feet where needed.
- On RCC or brick platforms (common in Indian kitchens), the top surface should be flat, cured and dry.
- Support the stone along its full length. Long unsupported spans between cabinets, and corners around sinks and hobs, are where tops crack.

### Overhangs

Granite can project beyond its support, but not far. A common guide in the stone industry:

| Thickness | Overhang without extra support |
|---|---|
| 18–20 mm | up to about 150 mm (6 in) |
| 30 mm | up to about 250 mm (10 in) |

Longer overhangs, for example breakfast bars, need steel brackets or corbels underneath. Ask your fitter to confirm for your stone and layout.

### Cut-outs

- Cut sink and hob openings from the maker's template, with the stone fully supported on both sides of the cut.
- **Round the inside corners** of every cut-out (a radius of at least about 10 mm). Sharp inside corners are where cracks start.
- Leave the gap around a hob that the hob maker asks for: hobs get hot and the stone must not be pinched.
- Polish or seal the cut edges of sink openings, which stay wet.
- Narrow strips left in front of or behind a sink cut-out are weak. Reinforce them from below (steel rods set in epoxy in grooves cut into the underside are a common method) and handle the piece with extra care until it's fixed.

### Fixing

- Fix the top to the cabinets or platform with dabs of **silicone or a stone adhesive** at regular spacing, not with cement mortar on wooden cabinets.
- On masonry platforms, use a full, even bed of suitable stone adhesive or mortar, with no hollow spots.

### Joints (seams)

- Keep joints **tight and level**, typically 1–2 mm.
- Fill with **colour-matched epoxy** or stone resin, then scrape and polish flush.
- Use seam-setting clamps to bring both pieces level before the epoxy sets.

### Splashback and edges

- Fix splashback strips with silicone or stone adhesive.
- Seal the joint between top and wall, and around the sink, with **neutral-cure silicone** made for natural stone. Some silicones stain stone; check the label.
- Finish exposed edges to the profile you chose (straight, half-bullnose, bevel) and polish them to match the face.

## 4. Floors

### The base

- The sub-floor must be **sound, clean, cured and flat**. Remove loose plaster, oil and dust.
- New concrete or screed needs time to cure and dry before stone goes on it; follow the time given for the mix used.

### Fixing method

| Method | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Cement mortar bed** (traditional) | Usual for thick slabs on new floors in India. Use a full, solid bed and tamp each stone down; avoid hollow spots. Pour a cement slurry on the bed before placing the stone so it bonds. |
| **Stone tile adhesive** (thin bed) | For calibrated tiles of even thickness on a flat base. Use an adhesive rated for natural stone and large tiles, spread with a notched trowel, and **back-butter** each tile so it's fully bedded. |

Whichever method is used, the stone must be **fully bedded**: tap it, and listen for hollow sounds. Hollow spots crack later under load.

### Joints and movement

- Keep **even joints**, typically 2–3 mm for polished granite. Use spacers.
- Leave **movement gaps** around the edge of the room and at doorways, hidden under the skirting, and add movement joints in large floors and where the floor meets a different base. Fill these with a flexible sealant, not grout.
- Check for **lippage** (one stone higher than the next) with a straightedge as you go; fix it before the bed sets.

### Grout

- Use a grout suited to natural stone and the joint width, in a colour close to the stone.
- Clean grout off the surface straight away; don't let it dry on polished granite.

### After laying

- Keep traffic off until the bed has set: follow the mortar or adhesive maker's time.
- Protect the finished floor with cardboard or sheets while other trades work.
- Large floors are sometimes ground and re-polished after laying to level the joints. Agree this with the fitter in advance.

---

## 5. Stairs

- **Treads** (the steps) are commonly 30 mm, or 18–20 mm on a full solid bed; **risers** (the upright faces) are often 15–20 mm.
- Fix each tread on a full bed with no hollow spots, sloping very slightly forward so water runs off.
- The front edge (**nosing**) takes the most wear. Round or chamfer it, and for outdoor or wet steps use a **flamed, leather or honed** finish or anti-slip grooves instead of a full polish, which is slippery when wet.

---

## 6. Sealing

Granite is dense, and many granites, especially dark ones, absorb very little water. Use the water test from the [Quality Inspection Guide](quality-inspection-guide.md) after installation:

- **Water beads and leaves no mark after 10–15 minutes:** sealing is usually not needed.
- **Water darkens the stone:** apply a penetrating (impregnating) sealer made for natural stone, following its label. Kitchen tops matter most, because of oil, turmeric, tea and wine.

Wipe off all excess sealer; sealer left on the surface dries to a hazy film. Repeat the water test every year or so and re-seal when water starts to darken the stone.

---

## 7. Daily care

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Wipe spills promptly, especially oil, turmeric, wine, tea and coffee | Use acid cleaners (vinegar, lemon, toilet or bathroom acid cleaners) |
| Clean with warm water and a **pH-neutral** stone cleaner or a mild dish soap | Use bleach, ammonia or abrasive scouring powders and pads |
| Use chopping boards and trivets | Put very hot pans straight from the stove onto the stone, cut directly on it, or stand on tops |
| Use mats at outside doors to keep grit off floors | Drag heavy furniture across floors |

Stains that have soaked in can often be drawn out with a **poultice** made for natural stone. Chips and cracks can usually be repaired with colour-matched epoxy by a stone fitter.
